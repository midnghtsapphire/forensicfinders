# Insect traces as search and evidence indicators (registry 2210)

**Status: PROTOTYPE / RESEARCH NOTE.** This is a literature summary only. It has no code, it makes no claimed results, and none of it is field-validated for this project. Sources were checked on 2026-10-21 (Mountain Time). Each one is listed in `sar/data/sources.json` (2200).

Scope: what insects leave behind at a death or burial site, how long those traces may persist, and which ones a searcher could notice by eye, by camera or by soil sampling. This is forensic science about remains, not about living people. It adds no people-search or background-check work.

## 1. Fly puparia (pupal casings)

- Blowfly larvae finish feeding, pupate, and leave empty **puparia** that can stay at or near the site after the adults leave. Gaudry (2009) reviews insect colonisation of buried remains. doi:[10.1007/978-1-4020-9684-6_13](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4020-9684-6_13).
- **Human DNA in empty puparia:**
  - Mazzanti et al. (2010) studied DNA degradation in empty puparia and the limits of genetic identification. *Forensic Sci Int* 195:99–102. doi:[10.1016/j.forsciint.2009.11.022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.forsciint.2009.11.022).
  - Marchetti et al. (2013) report human DNA extraction from empty puparia. *Forensic Sci Int* 229:e26–e29. doi:[10.1016/j.forsciint.2013.03.043](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.forsciint.2013.03.043).
- **Ageing and weathering of puparia:** Sharif et al. (2023) followed how cuticular hydrocarbons in *Lucilia sericata* empty puparia degrade over time. *Forensic Chemistry* 35:100519. doi:[10.1016/j.forc.2023.100519](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.forc.2023.100519).

## 2. Larvae: gut contents, artefacts and toxicology

- **Human DNA from maggot gut contents:**
  - Wells et al. (2001) obtained human mtDNA from the dissected gut of a maggot that had fed on human tissue. *J Forensic Sci* 46:685–687. doi:[10.1520/JFS15022J](https://doi.org/10.1520/JFS15022J).
  - Zehner et al. (2004) typed human STRs from the gut of larvae that fed on bodies with postmortem intervals of up to four months. *J Forensic Sci* 49. doi:[10.1520/JFS2003248](https://doi.org/10.1520/JFS2003248).
  - Campobasso et al. (2005) review gut-content analysis as a way to link a maggot to the body it fed on. *Am J Forensic Med Pathol* 26:161–165. doi:[10.1097/01.paf.0000163832.05939.59](https://doi.org/10.1097/01.paf.0000163832.05939.59).
  - Di Luise et al. (2008) genotyped human nuclear DNA from the larval gut. *FSI: Genetics Suppl* 1:591–592. doi:[10.1016/j.fsigss.2007.10.075](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.fsigss.2007.10.075).
- **Fly artefacts ("fly spots": regurgitate and faeces, the fly equivalent of frass):**
  - Durdle et al. (2011) tracked how the human DNA content of *Lucilia cuprina* artefacts changes over time. doi:[10.1016/j.fsigss.2011.09.007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.fsigss.2011.09.007).
  - Durdle et al. (2013) measured human DNA in artefacts from flies fed blood, semen and saliva. *Forensic Sci Int* 233:212–219. doi:[10.1016/j.forsciint.2013.09.015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.forsciint.2013.09.015).
  - Langer and Illes (2015) review how fly artefacts confound bloodstain pattern analysis, and the visual, contextual and chemical ways to tell them apart. doi:[10.1080/00085030.2015.1083306](https://doi.org/10.1080/00085030.2015.1083306).
- **Entomotoxicology** (drugs and toxins in insects that fed on remains):
  - Introna, Campobasso and Goff (2001) is the standard review. *Forensic Sci Int* 120:42–47. doi:[10.1016/S0379-0738(01)00418-2](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0379-0738(01)00418-2).
  - Campobasso et al. (2004) compared drug levels in blowfly larvae with levels in human tissues. *Int J Legal Med* 118. doi:[10.1007/s00414-004-0448-1](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00414-004-0448-1).
  - Neither source supports reading a dose from larval levels. Treat insect toxicology as qualitative (present or absent) unless a lab says otherwise.
- **Post-feeding dispersal:** Gomes, Godoy and Von Zuben (2006) review how larvae leave the body to pupate, which is why puparia can turn up away from the remains. *Naturwissenschaften* 93:207–215. doi:[10.1007/s00114-006-0082-5](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00114-006-0082-5).

## 3. Beetle frass

- *Dermestes* beetles dominate the dry-decay and skeletal stages. Magni et al. (2015) review their biology and how to collect them. *J Med Entomol* 52:755–769. doi:[10.1093/jme/tjv106](https://doi.org/10.1093/jme/tjv106).
- **Drugs in beetle frass:** Drumm, Goff, Scott and Moran (2016), AAFS abstract K25 ([PDF](https://www.aafs.org/sites/default/files/media/documents/AAFS-2016-K25.pdf)), detected methamphetamine, amphetamine and norephedrine in dermestid larvae, pupae, adults and frass after the beetles fed on dosed, buried rat remains. **Conference abstract, not peer-reviewed, and an animal model.**

## 4. What is detectable, and how

| Trace | By eye | By camera | By soil or sample |
|---|---|---|---|
| Empty puparia | Yes, as small brown barrel-shaped cases in soil, litter or clothing (§1) | Possible on close-up images. No published detector was found, so this is **untested** | Collect for species ID and possible human DNA (Mazzanti 2010; Marchetti 2013) |
| Live larvae / maggot masses | Yes, while present | **Thermal**: Johnson & Wallman (2013) used infrared imaging to document maggot-mass surface temperatures on pig carcasses ([10.1080/00450618.2013.793740](https://doi.org/10.1080/00450618.2013.793740)). Heaton et al. (2014) found lab masses 2.5–14 °C above ambient ([10.1111/1556-4029.12396](https://doi.org/10.1111/1556-4029.12396)). Gbenonsi & Higley (2025) review mass thermal ecology ([10.3390/insects16101018](https://doi.org/10.3390/insects16101018)). None of these tested detection from a drone or at search range | Gut contents can carry human DNA (§2). Larvae can be used for toxicology |
| Fly artefacts (spots) | Yes, on indoor surfaces | Ordinary photos for documentation (Langer & Illes 2015) | Swab for human DNA (Durdle 2011, 2013) |
| Dermestid frass | Yes, as powdery or beaded material in late decay | Not studied | Toxicology (AAFS 2016 abstract only) |

## 5. Crawl, walk, run (staged use in this project)

- **Crawl (now):** use this note as background in searcher briefings and the evidence checklist. Teach searchers to recognise puparia and frass and to photograph and flag them, not collect them. No code.
- **Walk:**
  - Add an optional, off-by-default "insect trace observed" tag to field logs so finds can be mapped next to existing layers.
  - Test whether the `sar/vision` template baseline can find puparia on labelled close-up photos. This needs a labelled image set first.
- **Run:** only after the walk stage succeeds and an accredited lab partner is in place. Then send samples (puparia, larvae, artefacts) through a proper chain of custody for species ID, human DNA and toxicology. The project itself does no lab claims.

## 6. Still needs field validation

- How long puparia and frass persist in local soils and climates, outdoors and when buried. The sources above are largely lab work, case reports or animal models.
- Whether thermal imaging can detect maggot masses from the air, at what range, and under canopy. No published test was found.
- Camera or vision detection of puparia. No dataset or published detector was found.
- Whether human DNA can be recovered from weathered puparia and artefacts at realistic intervals, given the degradation limits shown by Mazzanti 2010.
- Beetle-frass toxicology beyond one conference abstract.
