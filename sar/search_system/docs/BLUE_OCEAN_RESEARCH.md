# Adjacent-Tech Research (verified by the original author 2026-10-11; not re-checked in the port - confirm before citing)

## HIGH patent space
1. Gravesoil necrobiome PBI/PTI engine. Frontiers Microbiology 2025 review
   (forensic ecogenomics): soil microbial community shift marks burial (PBI)
   AND translocation (PTI - explicitly 'a new concept', 'relatively little
   attention'). Human Bacteroides persisted 198 days (Cobaugh 2015); microbial
   disturbance measurable 4 years (Keenan 2018); faint gravesoil signature at
   10 years (Burcham 2021); post-exhumation PTI discriminated at 120 days
   (Olakanye & Ralebitso-Senior 2022). Extends our eDNA layer from 'human DNA
   present' to 'burial clock + moved-body detection'.
2. Adipocere dielectric fingerprint. GPR detects grave-wax fatty-acid matrix
   'for decades or longer' (Screening Eagle GS8000 program; Florida Spodosol
   NIJ study detected controlled graves at 30 months). Pringle 10-year study:
   naked burial GPR-visible ~6yrs (weak), tarp-wrapped 10yrs; ERT complements
   (naked low-resistivity 4yrs, wrapped high-resistivity 10yrs). A dielectric
   signature library for cold cases is unclaimed space.

## LOW patent space - cite, integrate, do NOT claim
3. Muon tomography. Benhammou et al., J. Appl. Phys. 138, 084504 (2025):
   first archaeological demo (Jerusalem), ~30 m range; inventors themselves
   cite 1960s pyramid surveys as prior art. Relevant only for deep vaults
   beyond GPR reach.
4. LABRADOR field e-nose - VERIFIED (2026-10-11). Real, but government-originated
   and patented: ORNL invention (inventors Arpad A. Vass & Marcus B. Wise,
   U.S. application 12/848,118 filed 2010-07-31, "Light Weight Analyzer for
   Odor Recognition"), licensed Feb 2012 to Agile Technologies of Knoxville.
   FBI funded the underlying cadaver-odor database (ORNL + UT Decay Research
   Facility); NIJ funded the detector (12 heated metal-oxide sensors,
   $1,000-1,500/unit, <60 s qualitative field read comparable to GC-MS
   headspace). Never became a mainstream commercial product - do not expect
   to buy one. STATUS FOR SPEC: prior art (Vass/Wise 2010) - cite, never
   claim. Our value-add stays the fusion layer.
5. GPR/ERT dual-method surveys: established practice (Pringle 2016; MDPI
   Remote Sensing 2021 review). Use the 10-year detectability table to pick
   sensors per case, not as a claim.

## Practical notes for the field layer
- Seasonal moisture swings grave GPR visibility (Florida: wet months clearer).
- Clay masks burials after ~6 months (Schultz); sandy Spodosol retains signal.
- Cadaver dogs lose utility 15+ years out (VOC depletion) - exactly where
  adipocere-GPR and necrobiome layers take over.
